▼ Role of a Corporate Treasurer

A Corporate Treasurer manages a company's liquidity, currency exposure, and financial risk. Key responsibilities include:

• Cash Management — Ensure each subsidiary has enough local currency to operate

• FX Risk Management — Hedge foreign currency exposure to reduce P&L volatility

• Cash Concentration — Sweep excess cash to a central pool to optimize interest

• Liquidity Planning — Forecast cash flows and avoid expensive overdrafts

▼ FX Exposure & Hedging

Transaction Exposure: When a subsidiary has net cash flows in a foreign currency, their USD value fluctuates with exchange rates.

Translation Exposure: The USD value of foreign cash balances changes as FX rates move — this creates P&L volatility even without any cash movement.

Forward Contracts: Lock in an exchange rate today for a future cash flow. The hedge ratio determines what % of exposure is protected.

Hedge P&L = Qty × (Locked Rate − Spot Rate)

If you hedge 60% of EUR exposure, 60% of the cash flow is immune to FX moves. The remaining 40% floats with the market.

▼ Cash Sweeps & Liquidity

A cash sweep moves excess cash from subsidiary accounts to a central treasury pool (or vice versa).

Why sweep? Idle cash in subsidiaries earns no interest. Concentrated cash can be invested or used to cover deficits elsewhere. Overdrafts are expensive.

Sweep Threshold: If a sub's cash exceeds its target + threshold, the excess is swept to the central pool. If below target − threshold, cash is swept in.

The central pool earns interest on positive balances and charges interest on negative balances (borrowing).

▼ P&L Attribution

Total Treasury P&L decomposes into:

1. FX Translation — USD value change of cash balances due to FX rate moves

2. Hedge Settlement — P&L from maturing forward contracts (locked vs spot rate)

3. Overdraft Fees — Penalty when a subsidiary's cash goes negative

4. Interest Income — Interest earned on central pool balance

The treasurer's goal: minimize FX losses + overdraft fees while keeping hedging costs reasonable.

▼ The Hedge Ratio Dilemma

Hedging reduces uncertainty but has a cost (forward points).

0% hedge: Full exposure to FX moves. If the currency moves against you, P&L takes a hit. If it moves in your favor, you profit.

100% hedge: No FX exposure. P&L is predictable, but you pay the full forward cost and cap any upside.

Partial hedge (e.g. 60%): Balances protection with flexibility. The optimal ratio depends on your risk tolerance and view on currency direction.

In this simulator, you set a hedge ratio per currency. Watch how it affects total P&L vs P&L volatility.

▼ Scenario Guide

🎯 EM Crisis: BRL drops 20% instantly. If unhedged, the Brazilian subsidiary's USD value collapses.

🎯 EUR Weakens: EUR drops 8% against USD. Your largest non-USD sub takes a hit.

🎯 Risk-Off: All EM currencies sell off, safe havens rally. Tests your diversified hedge strategy.

🎯 Liquidity Crunch: A major customer delays payment. Multiple subsidiaries face cash shortfalls simultaneously.
